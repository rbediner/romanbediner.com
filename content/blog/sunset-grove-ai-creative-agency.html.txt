<p>Sunset Grove had a small-business marketing problem. Its vacation homes in Wake Forest are listed on Airbnb and Vrbo. The business needed to give more people a reason to book a stay, and keep doing the work that makes that happen.</p>

<p>That work extends well beyond posting a photograph. Who is planning a group trip? What brings them to the area? What should a campaign say to a family coming for a wedding, or a group visiting for a sporting event? Which photographs tell that story honestly? How does an idea become a week of finished content, and what should change when the results come back?</p>

<p>A full creative agency could take on those responsibilities. The project took a different approach: building an AI creative agency for Sunset Grove.</p>

<p>The product is Maya: a client-facing Chief Marketing Officer backed by a team of defined AI employee roles, shared business knowledge and a production system using Claude, Codex and Gemini. PULSE, the animated view in this article, is one interface into that larger product. The reason for building Maya was the marketing work Sunset Grove needed done.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="sell-the-reason-for-the-trip"><h2 id="sell-the-reason-for-the-trip">Sell the reason for the trip</h2>

<p>The operating brief gives the agency a useful direction: sell occasions and shared experiences, rather than mattresses, empty rooms and photographs of sofas.</p>

<p>Sunset Grove&#x27;s combined stay can accommodate a group of 13. That gives the agency a specific job. It needs to help the right guests see how staying together could fit their trip, using the real property and relevant local experiences to make the case.</p>

<p>Consider a family gathering around a wedding. A useful campaign would connect the occasion to the stay: space for the group, time together between events and a clear path to learn more. The local research, images and copy need to support that idea. This is an illustration of the agency&#x27;s assignment, not a reported campaign result.</p>

<p>The documented weekly plan included 17 placements for organic Instagram and Facebook. The intended path runs from that content to the website and then to Airbnb or Vrbo listing interest. Each step needs its own measurement. A listing click cannot be reported as a booking, and a popular post cannot be reported as revenue.</p>

<p>For a small business, keeping that work moving is a substantial responsibility. Someone has to decide what to make, coordinate its production, check the result, handle revisions and learn enough to improve the next week.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="build-the-agency-the-client-would-expect-to-hire"><h2 id="build-the-agency-the-client-would-expect-to-hire">Build the agency the client would expect to hire</h2>

<p>The project&#x27;s original product definition is unusually direct: Maya should be the creative agency the client works with. Showing the client the agency&#x27;s internal machinery would not satisfy that brief.</p>

<p>Sunset Grove should be able to talk with Maya about strategy, ask how marketing is performing, look at the work and request a change. Maya should bring a recommendation and explain the tradeoff. The business should not have to discover which AI worker is waiting for another worker, or move instructions between them.</p>

<p>An October 19 project record makes the gap concrete: the engine had made, independently accepted and scheduled all 17 posts, yet the documented assessment was that the client experience still failed. Work moving through a pipeline did not give the client the agency relationship they wanted.</p>

<p>That distinction shaped the product around six client needs: discussing strategy, understanding system health, judging content quality, interpreting results, having a real conversation and reviewing or changing creative. It also explains why adding another status dashboard would miss the point.</p>

<p>The product brief describes one responsive application centered on Maya. Its scope connects audience and event research, campaign recommendations, creative briefs, production, quality checks, scheduling, performance review and experiments. The client sees the choices that need their judgment. Routine coordination stays with the agency.</p>

<p>The important design decision is to organize the work around what Sunset Grove needs from an agency. The client experience is the standard the implementation has to meet.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="give-the-ai-employees-actual-jobs"><h2 id="give-the-ai-employees-actual-jobs">Give the AI employees actual jobs</h2>

<p>Maya&#x27;s documented team has 16 employee roles. These are defined responsibilities, not 16 interchangeable chatbots.</p>

<p>All 16 report to Maya. The Operations Manager coordinates handoffs rather than becoming a second management hierarchy.</p>

<p>This roster goes beyond a proposed organization chart. The October 27 deployment record reports the employee procedures and bounded CMO management layer installed in production, with a live Maya response that could identify the roles and retrieve the copywriter’s working procedure. That record documents a specific part of the build. It does not prove that all 16 responsibilities run autonomously from end to end.</p>

<p>The role names matter because a marketing assignment contains different kinds of judgment. A researcher has to establish whether a local recommendation is accurate. A copywriter has to connect it to the audience. A creative reviewer has to decide whether the resulting asset works. A publishing manager has to ensure the accepted version is the version that goes out.</p>

<p>Claude, Codex and Gemini provide execution capacity behind those roles. A role is not a promise of a separate model call, and the roster does not mean every post requires 16 calls. The organization defines who owns each responsibility; the execution system decides how the work is carried out.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="the-difficult-part-is-getting-the-work-to-travel"><h2 id="the-difficult-part-is-getting-the-work-to-travel">The difficult part is getting the work to travel</h2>

<p>Adding several AI systems creates coordination problems that a single prompt never has to solve. They need the same property facts and current brief. They need to know which asset version they are changing. A handoff needs to survive a delayed worker, a failed quality check or an edit that arrives after scheduling.</p>

<p>The build also had coordination problems among the AIs developing the system. Its architecture record describes four rejected pushes to a shared code branch in an hour and a rebase that broke Drive synchronization. The architecture called for separate working branches and one shared production ledger. Giving several AI systems access to the same work had created another operational problem to solve.</p>

<p>Without clear coordination, the person who wanted an agency becomes its project manager.</p>

<p>The design uses a shared message bus to carry assignments, acknowledgments and handoffs. In ordinary terms, it gives the workers a common way to pass work and report what happened. A separate production record connects a publishing slot to its brief, worker, creative version, review and publishing evidence.</p>

<p>Imagine Sunset Grove asks Maya to replace the opening image in a video. The agency has to identify the right video, route the edit to its producer and keep an outdated version from going out. The replacement then needs review. If the post was already scheduled, the change must reach that same publishing record rather than create a duplicate.</p>

<p>The client made one reasonable request. Behind it sit version control, routing, review and publication checks. Those are workflow requirements, not a claim that every recovery path has been demonstrated. They explain where much of the engineering effort goes when an AI tool becomes an agency service.</p>

<p>A useful operating rule is that the worker producing an asset cannot approve its own run. A separate reviewer needs the brief, the asset and the acceptance criteria, with authority to reject it. Approval belongs to a particular version. Changing that version means checking the replacement.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="keep-the-property-recognizable"><h2 id="keep-the-property-recognizable">Keep the property recognizable</h2>

<p>The agency also needs boundaries on what it can create.</p>

<p>For Sunset Grove, a beautiful image is a bad advertisement if the guest arrives to find a different room. The creative rules begin with real property photography. AI can help produce the marketing without inventing a kitchen, relocating windows or redesigning the property. Named local destinations need authentic, appropriately sourced imagery too.</p>

<p>This is where shared business knowledge becomes practical. The agency needs current property facts, approved assets, creative standards and prior decisions before it starts another piece of work. The client should not have to repeat the same correction to three different AI systems.</p>

<p>Quality review then has to connect those facts to the finished asset. Is the claim accurate? Does the opening make the campaign idea clear? Can someone read the text on a phone? Does the caption belong with the image? Has the accepted version survived the handoff to publishing?</p>

<p>Those questions belong in the service Sunset Grove receives. File generation is only one part of it.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="learn-from-the-marketing-without-inventing-the-result"><h2 id="learn-from-the-marketing-without-inventing-the-result">Learn from the marketing, without inventing the result</h2>

<p>The intended learning loop links performance to the next brief. Observe what happened, form a hypothesis, try a change and measure again. Keep enough context to distinguish a promising signal from an established lesson.</p>

<p>The same discipline applies to business outcomes. Reach, engagement, website visits and listing clicks can help the agency decide what to investigate. Claiming increased bookings requires booking evidence. Claiming revenue requires revenue evidence. Neither comes from an animated interface or a completed production job.</p>

<p>Maya was built to take on the work of a creative agency for a small business. The records include installed employee procedures, a management layer and a historical completed production cycle; they do not justify declaring the entire service fully autonomous or its commercial results proven.</p>

<p>For another business owner, the useful question is what responsibility they would otherwise need to hire for. Sunset Grove needed a sustained marketing service, from understanding the guest to producing, reviewing and improving the work. Building that service with AI meant defining the team and engineering the handoffs, while keeping a person responsible for consequential decisions.</p>

<p>The result to judge is the service the business receives. Can it discuss a campaign, get good work back, make a change and understand what happened next, without managing the machinery itself?</p>
