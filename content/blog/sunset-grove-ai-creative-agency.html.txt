<p>Sunset Grove had a familiar marketing problem. Their two vacation homes in Wake Forest are listed on Airbnb and Vrbo. They needed to give prospective guests a reason to choose those homes, then keep that marketing going week after week.</p>

<p>A listing gives someone a place to book. The work around it is broader: understanding the audience, finding relevant local experiences, developing campaigns, producing content and learning what brings people closer to a booking.</p>

<p>For a small hospitality business, hiring a creative agency is one way to take that work off the team&#x27;s plate. The project explored building an AI-powered agency to handle that work, with a lead to talk to, distinct production responsibilities and a reliable review process.</p>

<p>The design brings together Claude, Codex and Gemini under Maya, the client-facing agency lead. The hard part is making their work add up to a service the business can depend on.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="start-with-the-marketing-job"><h2 id="start-with-the-marketing-job">Start with the marketing job</h2>

<p>Airbnb and Vrbo marketing needs to help guests picture the stay. For Sunset Grove, that includes explaining how two homes can work together for a group and giving people a useful sense of the surrounding area.</p>

<p>A photograph of a living room has a job to do. So does a short video about a nearby outing. Each piece needs an audience, a reason to exist and a clear connection to the property. Producing attractive files without those decisions leaves the business with more content to manage.</p>

<p>The agency brief therefore covers the whole assignment: strategy, local research, copy, production, review, publishing and performance analysis. That is the scope a business would expect to discuss when hiring an agency. It is also the scope an AI agency has to take responsibility for.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="give-the-business-one-lead-to-work-with"><h2 id="give-the-business-one-lead-to-work-with">Give the business one lead to work with</h2>

<p>Maya is designed to be that point of contact. Sunset Grove should be able to discuss an idea, review the creative, request a change and understand what happened after publication. They should not have to relay instructions between three AI systems.</p>

<p>The project brief records an important gap in an earlier version: production status was visible, but the client experience still needed work. A dashboard can show that several tasks are running while leaving the business unsure what it will receive or when a decision is needed.</p>

<p>An agency lead needs to close that gap. When an asset is late or a review fails, the useful response is an explanation, a recommendation and a clear next step. Routine coordination belongs inside the agency.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="divide-the-work-without-multiplying-the-overhead"><h2 id="divide-the-work-without-multiplying-the-overhead">Divide the work without multiplying the overhead</h2>

<p>The documented production model assigns still-image work to Codex and Gemini, with Claude handling video assembly and music. Maya&#x27;s coordination role covers the assignment, available capacity and the rules for the job. These are choices for this project, rather than a claim that one model is universally best at a particular medium.</p>

<p>Sixteen defined agency roles cover responsibilities such as strategy, copywriting, research and publishing. They are procedures and areas of accountability. A worker can perform several roles; the design does not require sixteen model calls for every post.</p>

<p>Review has a separate owner. The worker that creates an asset cannot approve its own run. A fresh reviewer receives the asset, brief and rubric, and checks whether the work meets the assignment. Where the policy permits it, that reviewer can be another session of the same AI system. Independence comes from a separate review, with evidence and authority to reject the work.</p>

<p>That is a more useful way to assess an AI team than counting its agents. Someone has to own the result, and someone else has to be able to challenge it.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="use-a-message-bus-to-carry-the-handoffs"><h2 id="use-a-message-bus-to-carry-the-handoffs">Use a message bus to carry the handoffs</h2>

<p>The message bus is the shared communication layer between workers. It carries requests, acknowledgments, progress updates and handoffs, so the business does not become the courier.</p>

<p>Try Wednesday launch, select a sample post and follow its path through production and review. The weekly workload selector changes the illustration, not a tested capacity limit. On a phone, open the full-window view for more room.</p>

<p>In Sunset Grove&#x27;s design, each request has an identity and a status. A separate record tracks the publishing slot, including its brief, assigned worker, version, review and publishing evidence. Those records make it possible to tell the difference between an assignment that was accepted and an asset that is ready.</p>

<p>Take a simple edit: Sunset Grove asks to replace the opening image in a video. The intended workflow routes the request to the responsible worker, identifies the version being changed and holds that version while the edit is made. The replacement goes back through review. If the post is already scheduled, the change should update the same publishing record rather than create a second post.</p>

<p>That example is a workflow requirement, not a claim that every recovery path has been proved. It does show why the coordination matters. Without it, the client ends up checking which version is current and whether a requested change actually happened.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="protect-the-truth-of-the-property"><h2 id="protect-the-truth-of-the-property">Protect the truth of the property</h2>

<p>Hospitality creative has a clear boundary: guests should recognize the place they arrive at.</p>

<p>Sunset Grove&#x27;s rules start with real property photography. Generated scenes cannot invent a different kitchen, move windows or redesign the rooftop. A named local destination needs authentic, appropriately sourced imagery. The creative can help guests imagine the experience without changing what is being advertised.</p>

<p>Review also has to cover more than file dimensions. Does the opening communicate the idea? Is the text readable on a phone? Does the image support the caption? Is the approved version the one being published?</p>

<p>The workflow ties approval to an exact version. If that version changes, the earlier approval no longer covers the replacement. It is a small operational rule with a direct effect on what the guest sees.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="make-learning-useful-and-keep-it-honest"><h2 id="make-learning-useful-and-keep-it-honest">Make learning useful, and keep it honest</h2>

<p>The intended learning loop is straightforward: observe a result, form a hypothesis, test it in a future brief and measure again. Each brief is meant to record the playbook version it used, connecting the observation to a later creative decision.</p>

<p>The November 9 playbook listed zero proven lessons. It retained signals worth testing, with qualifications about the sample and measurement timing. That is a useful limit to state. A few posts can suggest a direction without establishing a reliable strategy.</p>

<p>For Airbnb and Vrbo marketing, attribution needs the same care. A click through to a listing shows interest; it does not establish a booking. Reach and reactions describe attention, not revenue. The agency needs to preserve those distinctions when recommending the next campaign.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="judge-the-agency-by-a-complete-piece-of-work"><h2 id="judge-the-agency-by-a-complete-piece-of-work">Judge the agency by a complete piece of work</h2>

<p>Parts of the system are built, but the complete agency workflow still needs verification. The evidence does not yet justify claiming a fully autonomous agency or proven booking gains.</p>

<p>The useful test is one complete assignment: a clear objective, an accountable lead, a business-grounded brief, finished creative, independent review and a verified publication. The next assignment should be able to build on that record without asking the client to reconstruct it.</p>

<p>For a business considering whether to hire or build an AI creative agency, that is a practical standard. Ask to see the whole path from the brief to the published work, including a revision and what was learned afterward.</p>

<p>Three AI systems offer different ways to produce the work. The agency earns its place when the business can trust it to carry the assignment through.</p>
