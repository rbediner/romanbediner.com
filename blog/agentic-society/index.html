AI keeps changing. Your business still has to make money.

If you own a business or lead one, AI is already competing for a place on your agenda. Alongside customers, sales, delivery, cash flow, and your team, there is another decision to make: where should you invest in something that keeps changing?

You want your people to accomplish more. You want better customer experiences, stronger follow-through, and room to grow. The difficult part is connecting what AI can do to what your particular business needs next.

That is the conversation I want more CEOs and business owners to have. It is also the reason I want to introduce you to Agentic Society: a community of owners, builders, and AI advisors working on those questions together.

The business case for building together

You should have people to work through this with.

A CEO can find another AI demonstration almost anywhere. The more useful conversation starts when someone asks about the business behind the demonstration. Where does work get stuck? What does your team keep doing by hand? Which investment would make the biggest difference to your customers?

Agentic Society brings business owners and AI builders into a shared working community. Founded by Austin Distel, formerly CMO at Jasper, it combines a mastermind with practical implementation: people comparing approaches, working on their businesses, and helping one another get something useful into use.

The relationship is what gives that work continuity. An owner in another industry may recognize a handoff you have stopped noticing. A builder can help turn an ambitious idea into a manageable first version. An advisor can help you think through the next step when you get stuck.

Over time, those conversations have more context. People learn what you are trying to accomplish. You learn whose experience is relevant, which questions they ask, and how they approach a tradeoff. Staying current becomes more useful when you can discuss a development with people you know and connect it to a decision you actually need to make.

The build gives you something useful. The relationships give you people to keep building with.

Online and in Austin

A community you can stay part of between events.

You can participate from outside Austin. The current membership includes weekly online calls, access to AI advisors and the community in Slack, and a 90-day implementation path. Austin adds monthly member boardrooms and two two-day hackathons a year.

Each setting gives the relationship a different kind of time. A recurring call keeps you in the conversation. Slack lets a question continue after the call ends. A focused sprint gives an idea a place on your calendar. In person, boardrooms and hackathons offer time to build alongside people and get to know them.

You also bring something valuable: an understanding of your own company. You know where customers hesitate, where employees lose time, and what a better result would mean. You do not need to arrive with a finished AI strategy. You do need to participate, or put someone on your team in charge of the build.

Membership is a done-with-you community. That makes the working relationship important. Confirm the current program and event details with the team as you consider how you would take part.

What could you build together?

Start with a problem your business already has.

Perhaps the same questions keep finding their way back to the owner. Perhaps sales follow-ups depend on someone remembering to send them. Perhaps a capable team loses part of its week gathering context before it can begin the real work.

Those are useful starting points for a conversation with other owners and builders. They lead to different possibilities: a Company Brain your team can consult, an AI employee with a defined responsibility, a workflow that connects a customer request to the next person who needs to act.

Two public member stories on the Agentic Society website make those possibilities more concrete. AZ Moyer, a CPA, describes putting his firm's procedures and frequently asked questions into a Company Brain so his team can find consistent answers. Adelle Archer, co-founder and CEO of Eterneva, talks about building a morning briefing, email triage, and a communications system. Read their accounts on Agentic Society's website.

The work starts to feel relevant when you can picture your own team using it. What information would they need? Who would use the result? What could they do with the capacity it creates?

One example from my work

A Company Brain. An AI team. People leading the business.

The systems I help build are one example of where this can lead. A Company Brain provides shared knowledge: the business context, processes, decisions, and working information that an AI employee needs to be useful. The team around it has distinct responsibilities.

Help the team remember and follow through.

The Project Manager helps turn conversation into visible commitments, owners, and follow-ups. The Chief of Staff organizes knowledge and prepares summaries and context. One follows the work; the other helps the organization retain what it knows. Together, those jobs support continuity between a discussion and what happens next.

Support growth, decisions, and useful learning.

The RevOps Engineer supports revenue, pipeline, and business-scenario questions. The AI Correspondent assesses developments so the team can consider what deserves attention. The Solution Architect develops a starting proposal around a business problem. These are inputs people can use when deciding what to test, fund, or improve.

Coordinate the work and keep improving it.

The Director of Fleet Orchestration and Engineering coordinates the agents. The Continuous Improvement Engineer looks for ways to improve reliability and efficiency. The Staff Engineer independently reviews proposed code changes. Coordination, improvement, and review each have a job alongside the agents doing the everyday work.

People still set priorities and own decisions. The value is in connecting company knowledge, defined responsibilities, and workflows to the business they serve. My work spans other systems as well; this example is a way to understand the approach, rather than a complete inventory.

If you want to explore the implementation, I have documented the AI employee fleet and build guide and a practical AI project manager guide. The public AI Employee Builder also shows how a role can begin with questions about the work you need done.

For a business owner considering Agentic Society, the larger opportunity is having people to build that understanding with. Someone else's first useful agent may give you an idea. Your own business context determines where you take it.

Make the capacity count

What will your business do with the time it gets back?

That is the next question I would bring into the community. If your team spends less time finding answers, could it respond to customers sooner? If project follow-through improves, could delivery become more dependable? If a specialist has fewer repetitive tasks, where could their expertise create more value?

A first build gives you something specific to discuss. Choose a recurring problem, give someone responsibility for it, and decide what you will watch: response time, missed follow-ups, rework, or the time spent gathering information. Then learn from what actually happens before expanding the investment.

Shared experience helps you keep the business in focus. You can bring back a result that surprised you, a team member's objection, or a workflow that did not work as expected. Other owners can help you examine the tradeoff. Builders can help you change the approach.

Is Agentic Society for you?

Bring curiosity, business context, and a willingness to contribute.

I would encourage CEOs and business owners to explore Agentic Society if they want to put AI to work and value learning alongside other people doing the same. Think about the problem you would bring, who would own the work, and how you would make time to participate.

Is Agentic Society for nontechnical business owners?

Yes. The current program says you do not need to be a technology founder. Someone needs to own the build, whether that is you or an operator you assign, with help available from advisors and the community.

Can I join Agentic Society online outside Austin?

There is a virtual component through online calls, advisors, training, and Slack. Austin hosts the in-person boardrooms and hackathons. Confirm the current schedule with the team.

Will someone build everything for me?

Membership is done-with-you. Separate done-for-you services are outside membership and can be discussed with the team.

What should I bring to a first conversation?

A business problem you would like to work on and a sense of what a better result would look like. An unfinished question is a useful place to begin.
