AI keeps changing. Your business still has to make money.

AI enablement is a business decision: where should you invest so your people can accomplish more?

More time for customers. Better follow-through. Less work spent finding information the company already knows. Those are useful starting points. The next question is what your business will do with that capacity: improve delivery, serve more customers, strengthen sales, or give a specialist room to do harder work.

Following every AI release will not answer that for you. You need your business context, a practical way to build, and people who can help you work through the decisions.

That is the connection I want to make between Agentic Society and the systems I help build as its Fractional COO: a Company Brain, AI employees with defined jobs, connected workflows, and human leadership. The technology matters because of the work it makes possible.

The value of the room

What is Agentic Society?

Agentic Society is a community and implementation program for business owners putting AI to work. It combines online participation with Austin gatherings, bringing owners, builders, and advisors into recurring conversations about the businesses they are actually running.

That combination matters. A tutorial can show you where to click. Another owner can help you ask whether the thing is worth building in the first place. A working session can expose a missing handoff, an unclear responsibility, or a customer experience you had not considered.

I want more of those conversations. Which problem is expensive enough to solve? What context does an AI employee need? Who checks the result? What will your team actually do with the time it gets back?

The relationships are part of the value. You get to know how other people think, what they are trying, and where they are getting stuck. Over time, that creates a more useful way to stay current than chasing every announcement alone.

From the workbench

Company Brain first. AI employees with real jobs.

The foundation is a Company Brain: a shared place for the context a business keeps having to explain. Around it, AI employees take on distinct responsibilities. A project manager makes commitments visible. A Chief of Staff turns scattered knowledge into context the team can use. Other agents support revenue operations, engineering, and specific business decisions.

These are examples of systems and tools we are developing around the business. They illustrate the approach; they are not a promise that every tool is included in membership or that a particular result is guaranteed.

Start with company knowledge

A Company Brain the work can build on.

An agent needs more than a clever prompt. It needs to understand the business: what the company does, what the team has decided, how work should happen, and which lessons should carry forward. Our Company Brain gives that context a shared home instead of leaving it scattered across individual conversations.

Think about a question your team answers repeatedly. A useful starting point is to capture the answer, its source, and who keeps it current. Then the next person or agent can start with context instead of starting over.

The useful output: shared business context that people can inspect, correct, and reuse.

Turn conversation into follow-through

A project manager that keeps commitments visible.

Our AI Project Manager works with action items, open loops, decisions, and blockers. It helps turn team conversation into a shared tracker: what was agreed, who owns it, and what needs attention. People still own the commitments and the decisions.

A request such as “I will send the proposal on Thursday” should not disappear into chat. It should become visible work with an owner, while an unclear request should become a question, not an invented promise.

The useful output: a clearer view of the work and the conversations needed to move it forward. See the project manager guide.

Keep the organization informed

A Chief of Staff that connects the knowledge.

The Chief of Staff has a different job: turn discussions and transcripts into organized Company Brain knowledge, prepare digests, and surface context the team can use. The project manager follows the work; the Chief of Staff helps the organization remember what it knows.

For example, after a working session, the next useful artifact may be a decision summary and updated context for the next conversation. That is different from simply adding more tasks to a list.

The useful output: knowledge that remains useful after the meeting ends, with human review of what is recorded and shared.

The wider team gives those jobs support. A Director of Fleet Orchestration and Engineering coordinates the agents and their work. A RevOps Engineer supports questions about revenue, pipeline, and business scenarios. A Continuous Improvement Engineer looks for reliability and efficiency improvements, while a Staff Engineer independently reviews proposed code changes. A Solution Architect prepares proposals for a business problem before people make the decision.

This is one operating example. My broader work includes creative operations and other business systems; one repository is not the whole inventory. The lesson to copy is the structure: shared context, distinct responsibilities, and clear human authority.

Arrive better prepared

A starting point for the room.

Our solution-preparation workflow uses business context and previous discussions to draft a starting proposal. The people in the room challenge it. What they actually decide becomes context for the next conversation.

The useful output: better preparation while people keep ownership of the answer.

A narrower supporting example is the AI news desk. It separates whether a development is credible from whether it changes a business decision. The principle is the same: give the agent useful context and a specific job, rather than ask it to generate more noise.

Give AI a real job

An employee builder that starts by interviewing you.

The public AI Employee Builder starts with questions about the role you need. Its structure covers identity, memory, routines, tools, and a launch gate. The Dashboard Builder follows a similar discipline: understand the need, write the requirements, then choose the appropriate build.

The useful output: a defined responsibility and a way to check whether the system is ready to use.

These examples share a pattern: bring business context, give the system a bounded job, and keep learning from the result. If you want the technical detail, my agentic AI employees guide and AI project manager guide explore the operating mechanics.

Bring a business problem

Connect AI investment to capacity, customers, and revenue.

Saving time is a beginning. The business value depends on what happens next. Does a faster follow-up help a prospect move forward? Does better preparation free a specialist to solve a harder problem? Does accessible company knowledge reduce repeated questions?

For example, consider a customer-facing team that loses time finding answers and chasing follow-ups. A Company Brain could make approved knowledge easier to reuse; a project manager could keep agreed follow-ups visible. That is an illustrative starting point, not a measured customer result. The test is whether the actual team can answer and follow through more reliably.

I would bring one recurring constraint into the community and define a small test before choosing a tool:

Your first conversation

The friction
What keeps slowing the business down?
The responsibility
Who owns the outcome and reviews the work?
The first build
What is the smallest useful change we can try?
The evidence
What would tell us it helped?

Then observe the real workflow. Measure something relevant: response time, rework, a missed handoff, or time spent gathering context. Give the freed capacity a purpose. Revenue growth is an ambition worth pursuing, but it is not an automatic consequence of adding AI.

This is where a community can make the work better. You can compare the problem with someone facing a similar constraint, pressure-test the approach, and return with what happened.

Make an informed choice

Is Agentic Society a fit for you?

It is worth exploring if you own or lead a business, want practical help adopting AI, and are willing to contribute context and time. You should want to learn alongside people who are doing the work, not just collect another library of content.

Is Agentic Society for nontechnical business owners?

The program is positioned for business owners. You need an understanding of your business and a willingness to participate. Technical complexity will depend on what you decide to build.

Can I join Agentic Society online outside Austin?

There is a virtual component through online calls and Slack. The current offer also includes in-person Austin boardrooms and hackathons. Confirm the current schedule and participation details with the team.

Will someone build everything for me?

The core program is done-with-you. Separate done-for-you services may be discussed with the team. If you need fully outsourced delivery, clarify that before joining.

What should I bring to a first conversation?

One recurring business problem, who it affects, and what an improvement would look like. You do not need a polished AI strategy to start asking useful questions.
